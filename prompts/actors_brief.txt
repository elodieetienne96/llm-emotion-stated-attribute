Emotions and intensities
This is an example. Feel free to choose the situation to best portray the requested emotion.

1. Hapiness: A positive emotional state characterized by joy and satisfaction.
   a. Low Intensity: It's like feeling a gentle warmth in the chest.
   b. High Intensity: It's like experiencing an uncontrollable urge to jump for joy.
   Examples:
   - Receiving a gift from friends and family that you've been wanting.
   - Getting a long-awaited promotion at work.
   - Winning an award or competition you worked hard to achieve.

2. Sadness: A negative emotional state marked by melancholy and emotional pain.
   a. Low Intensity: It's like having a lump in your throat.
   b. High Intensity: It's like feeling on the verge of an emotional breakdown.
   Examples:
   - Losing a loved one or a pet.
   - Receiving distressing news about a loved one's health.
   - Going through a painful breakup with a partner.

3. Fear: An emotion in response to a perceived threat or dangerous situation, causing anxiety.
   a. Low Intensity: It's like having a knot in your stomach.
   b. High Intensity: It's like feeling paralyzed.
   Examples:
   - Being alone in a dark forest and hearing strange noises.
   - Encountering a wild animal while hiking.

4. Disgust: An emotion of revulsion toward something unpleasant.
   a. Low Intensity: It's like sensing something unpleasant but tolerable.
   b. High Intensity: It's like feeling physical repulsion.
   Examples:
   - Discovering mold in food you were about to eat.
   - Walking through a dirty and foul-smelling street.
   - Meeting a person who doesn't take care of themselves and emits a strong body odor.

5. Anger: A negative emotional state characterized by irritation and frustration.
   a. Low Intensity: It's like feeling your blood pressure rise.
   b. High Intensity: It's like experiencing boiling, uncontrollable anger.
   Examples:
   - Getting stuck in traffic when you're running late for an important meeting.
   - Arguing with a close friend due to a misunderstanding.
   - Facing offensive comments or insults.

6. Surprise: A momentary emotion caused by something unexpected.
   a. Low Intensity: It's like momentarily being taken aback by unexpected news.
   b. High Intensity: It's like feeling completely stunned by a sudden and astonishing revelation.
   Examples:
   - Receiving an unexpected marriage proposal.
   - Winning the lottery.
   - Discovering a gift you didn't expect to receive.

7. Self-Confidence: A feeling of assurance and belief in one's own abilities and skills.
   a. Low Intensity: It's like quietly feeling confident in your abilities.
   b. High Intensity: It's like radiating an aura of absolute confidence.
   Examples:
   - Delivering a speech in front of a large audience with confidence.

8. Confusion: A mental state marked by a lack of clarity or understanding.
   a. Low Intensity: It's like trying to grasp a complex concept.
   b. High Intensity: It's like being completely lost and disoriented.
   Examples:
   - Receiving contradictory instructions at work.
   - Getting lost in a foreign city without a map or GPS.
   - Trying to understand a complex math problem without initial success.

9. Contempt: An emotion of disdain or superiority towards something or someone.
   a. Low Intensity: It's like disagreement or disapproval, but not eliciting a strong reaction.
   b. High Intensity: It's like completely ignoring someone's suggestions, openly ridiculing them, or expressing contempt by treating them condescendingly.
   Examples:
   - Displaying an arrogant and condescending attitude toward others.
   - Speaking disdainfully about your interests or values.

10. Sympathy: A positive emotion marked by understanding and affection for another person's emotions or situation.
   a. Low Intensity: It's like feeling a mild concern, some empathy, but not being deeply affected.
   b. High Intensity: It's like feeling genuine concern for the other person, sharing their emotions, and being deeply affected.
   Examples:
   - Supporting a friend going through a tough time.
   - Feeling compassion for someone sharing an emotional story.
